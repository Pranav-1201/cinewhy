## 2. What I found (all RAN unless marked)

**U1. The first-visit questions DO open automatically.** On a clean browser profile the sheet opened
(`#onboard-dialog.open` was true, `localStorage` empty). `main.js:91` opens it only while
`state.onboarded` is false, and **both** "Show me films" and "Skip" set that flag forever, in that
browser, per address. So on your machine it will never show again unless storage is cleared. The fix is
product design, not a bug: a profile you can re-open, and a first-run flow that is worth completing.

**U2. Half-width window.** At 800 px the search box is **110 px wide** while the three text buttons take
385 px (measured `getBoundingClientRect`). The only breakpoint (`cinewhy.css:308`, 640 px) is too low and
the buttons never collapse. At 375 px the "How it works" button is cut off and **the dark-mode toggle is
pushed fully off screen** (button right edges at 366 and 416 px in a 360 px page).

**U3. Duplicates across shelves.** "Avengers: Endgame" appears in both "Tonight's picks" and "New and now";
"Dune: Part Two" is the newest film shown for "New and now", a 2019 film also counts as new, because
"new" means "within 7 years of the newest film" and the newest in the catalogue is 2024.

**U4. Receipts.** A line like `Acting +0.54 (238 sentences)` shows a score on a minus-one-to-plus-one scale
that the page never explains, plus a bar and two quotes. A normal viewer cannot tell whether +0.54 is
good. "Criticism / Praise" under the bars is the only legend.

**U5. Language and era filters.** "Any year / New and now / Since 2012 / Since 2000" are fixed buckets.
Languages are English and Hindi only, enforced by a literal type in the API (`languages: 'en' or 'hi'`
returned 422 for `xx`, RAN).

**D1. Catalogue years (answers your 2023 question).** RAN on `artifacts/v3/meta.json`: 11,065 films,
9,858 English and 1,207 Hindi, years 1950 to 2025. Films per release year: 2019 339, 2020 323,
**2021 240, 2022 240, 2023 254, 2024 117, 2025 1**. Reviews: IMDb-side source (ebD) stops about 2020
(only 3 of the 852 films from 2021 on have any ebD reviews); Rotten Tomatoes carries 850 of those 852.
MovieLens ratings run to October 2023 (SOURCE: `DATASET.md`). So the honest cut-off is **2020 for
IMDb reviews and about 2023/2024 for everything else**; your "2023" is about right for ratings.

**D2. Hindi since 2021.** Only 21 Hindi films in the catalogue are from 2021 on (7, 4, 6, 4 for
2021-2024). Hindi films have no MovieLens signal and only 72 link to Rotten Tomatoes.

**D3. Candidate pools in the raw TMDB dump** (`TMDB_all_movies.csv`, 1,264,278 rows, RAN). Counting films
with an IMDb id, by **IMDb votes** (the catalogue's own popularity signal; TMDB's own vote counts are far
too thin for Indian cinema: only 17 Telugu films reach 100 TMDB votes):

| Language | IMDb votes at least 1,000 | at least 5,000 | at least 20,000 | from 2021 on, at least 1,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| English | 34,825 | 14,641 | 7,150 | 4,630 |
| Hindi | 2,037 | 1,010 | 353 | 560 |
| Telugu | 938 | 187 | 30 | 588 |
| Tamil | 877 | 252 | 51 | 392 |
| Japanese (all) | 1,681 | 490 | 140 | 192 |
| French | 2,958 | 848 | 213 | 396 |

Of Japanese films with at least 300 TMDB votes, 220 of 346 are Animation (RAN). English films from 2024,
2025, 2026 with at least 500 TMDB votes: 152, 122, 85 (2026 is a partial year).

**D4. The 4:3:2:1 ratio cannot be a catalogue ratio.** Hindi has about 2,000 candidates with 1,000 votes
and Telugu about 940; this already sets a ceiling of roughly 2,900 Hindi-bloc films before any review
coverage loss (the measured Hindi review link rate is about 66%, `DATASET.md`). At 4:3 that caps English at
about 3,900 films, so matching the ratio in the catalogue means **throwing away about 6,000 of your 9,858
English films**. See decision Q1: I recommend applying the ratio to what the visitor SEES.

**S1. Security / deploy facts.** The rate limiter counts `request.client.host` (SOURCE). Behind a reverse
proxy or HF/Render ingress that is the proxy address, so all visitors share 120 POSTs a minute; your target
design sends about 20 POSTs per page load.

**S2. Licences (SOURCE, `DATASET.md`).** Rotten Tomatoes reviews are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial,
share-alike); MovieLens forbids redistribution and `item_sim.npy` is derived from it; TMDB needs
attribution and is non-commercial unless licensed. A private demo is fine. **A public host redistributes
a derivative of MovieLens: needs a decision before deploy.**

## 4. Strategic direction (Phase 3, first)

Who uses this: someone choosing a film tonight who distrusts black-box rankings. A commercial API can rank
films better; **none shows the review sentences behind each pick.** The durable differentiator is the
receipt, a trust property, not the algorithm (D-018 says plainly that the aspects do not raise hit rate).
So every change below either makes the receipt easier to understand (Phase E), makes the picks more
personal (C, D, G) or widens what can be picked (F). No repositioning is needed.

## 6. Profile and "rate films" design (browser-first, accounts later)

Stored in `localStorage` as one versioned object (`cinewhy.profile.v3`), with **Export / Import as a JSON
file** so a person can move devices now and an account can adopt the same object later (your choice:
"browser now, accounts later"). Never leaves the browser unless a request carries ids.

Fields: `age_band`, `region`, `languages` (multi), `favourite_genres` (multi), `liked`, `disliked`,
`watched` (each optionally with a 1-5 rating), `want_to_watch`, filter choices, "hide watched" toggle.
Sex stays off (Q5, D-019).

First visit: one short sheet (languages, favourite genres, age, region) then **"Rate 12 films"**: cards that
show poster, title, year, one-line description and a star rating; buttons Like, Not for me, Skip, "I have
seen it". Cards are chosen to be informative: well known, spread across languages and taste clusters
(chosen by a fixed, tested rule, not at random). The same dialog opens from the home page and the profile
at any time ("Rate more films"). Phase C **measures** how many rated films it takes (0, 5, 10, 20 ratings
on held-out MovieLens users, same hit-at-20 protocol as D-018) and the card count is set from that curve,
not from a guess.

Profile page sections: personal details and preferences; Liked and Disliked lists (each film editable:
move, remove); Watched; Want to watch; Settings (the three tools from the old header). The first-run
sheet re-opens from Settings, and also appears whenever the profile is empty, so it can never be lost
the way it was in U1.

**Series (your request).** What is known (SOURCE, general knowledge, unverified here): TMDB has TV
endpoints and a TV reviews endpoint; IMDb publishes free non-commercial title files that include TV series.
What is certain from this repo (RAN/SOURCE): there are **no series ratings** (MovieLens is films only),
ebD matches only films by `Title (YYYY)` so series cannot match, the Rotten Tomatoes file is film-centric.
So series would have **no "viewers like you" signal and fewer receipts**; they could still be ranked by
IMDb rating, votes, genre and review text. That is why Phase I is a spike with a stop condition.

**Hindi "viewers like you".** Collaborative filtering cannot work for Hindi films until there are ratings,
and none exist in the data (RAN: only 72 Hindi films link to Rotten Tomatoes; few have MovieLens
ratings). Phase G replaces the missing signal with content similarity; the later, real fix is your own
users' likes and watched lists. No offline number can be reported for Hindi, so the About page will say
"not measured".

**Aspect finder.** Stays the weakest component. No fix is claimed here. The two failed recall fixes in
D-015 are not retried; the plan is independent labels first, then at most one new method with its pass
rule written down before it is run.

## 10. Deployment recommendation (free tier only)

Facts that decide it (SOURCE unless marked): one Python process; **490 MB** similarity matrix plus
**365 MB** of artifacts that must be present at start; about 30 s boot while hashes are verified; state is
in process memory (rate limiter) and in the browser (profile), so there is no database to host.

**Recommended: Hugging Face Spaces, Docker Space, free CPU Basic.** A web search this run (not the vendor
pricing page) reports the hardware as 2 vCPU, 16 GB RAM, 50 GB disk that is **not persistent**, free; the
same sources disagree on whether a free account may run a Docker Space, so confirm on the pricing page
first. Fits because the matrix is memory-mapped and 16 GB is ample. Cost: **$0 (unverified)**. The
non-persistent disk means artifacts are fetched at start from a dataset repo or release asset, which
Phase J already requires (P2).

**Alternative: Render free web service.** Search results (third-party) say 512 MB RAM, 0.1 CPU, spins down
after 15 minutes idle, about a minute to wake, ephemeral disk. **Ruled out as the primary** by one
architectural fact: a 490 MB memory-mapped matrix plus Python and numpy in 512 MB leaves no headroom, and
each wake-up re-verifies about 560 MB of files. **Serverless (Vercel, Lambda) and static hosts are ruled
out** by the same fact: the service needs a large file present at boot and server-side ranking, which
function size and time limits do not allow.

Whatever host: P3 (licence), P4 (forwarded client address), Phase A (a Dockerfile that builds), and a
drilled rollback come first.

## 11. Rules for implementing sessions

  for review.
* Stage explicit paths, never `git add -A`; run `git status --short` before every commit.
* Restructures are additive: new code lands and passes before old code is removed.
* Never edit a test to make a change pass; a failing guard test is the finding.
* No new dependency without asking (Q8 lists the two expected ones).
* Every shelf, filter and receipt rewrite needs a screenshot at 375 and 1440 px in the same message as
  the claim that it works.
* Numbers shown in the UI come from `eval.json` or the artifact, never typed in.
* Leave uncommitted work untouched; end each phase with fresh evidence from that session.
* Environment: venv Python by absolute path, `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8`, stub-then-write for new files
  (GateGuard), sweep 0-byte junk files after writing docs full of arrows.

### 15.3 Confidence weighting (Pranav's idea): what exists and how to test it

**What already exists (SOURCE, read this session):** aspect scores are shrunk toward zero by their sentence support, and popularity pulls IMDb ratings toward the mean with a 5,000-vote prior (`VOTE_PRIOR`, `discover.py`). So a film with few reviews already counts for less. What is **missing** is a weight for the number of reviews in the final ranking, and a rule that Tier B films cannot take the spotlight or the top of a shelf.

**Proposed experiment (Phase F, before shipping):** multiply each film's final score by a confidence `n / (n + k)` where `n` is its review count; compare hit-at-20 on the same 2,000 held-out MovieLens users with `k` in 10, 50, 200 (choose `k` on validation users only; evaluate the test users once; report intervals as D-018 and D-020 do). **Honest limit:** the held-out test can only score films that have MovieLens ratings, and Tier B films mostly do not, so it can show the weighting does not hurt English, but it cannot prove it helps Hindi. Expect a small effect; the real value is protection: no 30-review film topping a shelf.

  with at least 1,000 votes carry the Animation genre (715 at 5,000 or more): this includes Western cartoons as
  well as anime.
* **"Adult titles" in the explicit sense are a non-issue for popular data.** IMDb flags 25,876 titles as adult
  (its explicit-content flag), but **only 25 have at least 1,000 votes**. If "adult" meant mature-rated (18+
  certification), that is a different field (TMDB `certification_us`), and the 18+ label in the plan would come
  from there. Please say which you meant.
* **MovieLens belief 2024 is NOT a source of recent-film ratings (measured).** `ml_belief_2024.zip`
  (87,290,935 bytes, dated 2024-05-17, from `files.grouplens.org`; the GroupLens *website* times out here but
  its file server answers) holds 2,046,124 ratings from only **4,418 users**. After ML-32M's cut-off
  (2023-10-13) it has 302,326 ratings from 3,001 users, but only **10,734 ratings on the 3,843 films ML-32M
  lacks, and just 82 of those films have 20 or more ratings (17 have 100 or more)**. It carries no
  links file (movie ids, titles and genres only), so any join to our catalogue is by title and year. Its licence
  allows redistribution of the data and transformations under the same non-commercial terms, which is more
  permissive than ML-32M's, but there is too little in it to matter. The "latest" MovieLens file on the server
  is dated 2023-07-20, older than ML-32M. **Conclusion: no free MovieLens source adds ratings for 2024 to 2026
  films; recent films will need content similarity, IMDb ratings or Letterboxd (unverified).**
* **Consequence for the plan:** the candidate lists are not the bottleneck anywhere in English, series
  or recent years. The bottleneck is review text per title (section 14, section 16), which these dumps do not
  contain. `title.ratings` is the cheapest way to refresh vote counts for ranking.

review count. 91,019 films selected; 35,713 had any review; 7,813 linked to Rotten Tomatoes, 32,519 to ebD.

What this settles:
* **English reaches about 10,000 at the strict 50-review floor with no new data** (9,706 here against 7,886 in
  the shipped build), though only 861 of those are from 2021 on. Series are not in these dumps at all, so
  English series are additional and need a source (section 14).
* **The other four languages are not close, at any floor.** At 50 reviews: Hindi and Telugu together 355 against
  a target of 7,500, Japanese 147 against 5,000, French 171 against 2,500. Even the weak 5-review floor gives
  1,725, 799 and 1,286. These dumps are English-centric, so the Tier B (ratings-only) tier is not a nicety for
  these languages; it is the only way the catalogue can contain them at all in the near term.
* The 4:3:2:1 ratio therefore cannot be met with full-receipt titles from any known free source. If the ratio
  stays a hard requirement the catalogue for those languages is mostly Tier B, which is a product decision
  Pranav has already allowed (section 15.1), now with its size measured.
* **Title-matching lever, measured** (`experiments/title_join_lift.py`, 125,897 wide candidates; counts are
  links by normalised title+year, an upper bound because it does not check that a film reaches 50 reviews).
  Adding a second key from TMDB's `original_title`:

  | language | films | ebD by title | ebD extra via original title | RT by title | RT extra |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | English | 74,378 | 35,089 | +206 | 6,955 | 0 |
  | French | 19,043 | 3,799 | **+2,778 (+73%)** | 94 | 0 |
  | Japanese | 21,167 | 1,843 | +62 | 68 | +1 |
  | Hindi | 8,073 | 3,513 | +35 | 75 | 0 |
  | Telugu | 3,236 | 722 | +1 | 5 | 0 |

  Reading: original-title matching is worth building for **French only**. For Japanese, Hindi and Telugu the
  dumps simply do not hold these films' reviews under any title, so matching cannot fix them; they need new
  sources (TMDB reviews, MyAnimeList and so on), as section 15 says.
* **Two defects found on the way** (the first run overstated Japanese, 15,050 extra links, and Telugu, 908):
  1. `film_key` keeps only ASCII letters and digits, so a Japanese or Hindi title becomes the bare year
     (`'2001'`) and "links" to every film of that year. The experiment now ignores such keys. **Check the
     shipped build for the same flaw**: any non-Latin title there would match on year alone.
  2. `film_key` also drops accented letters (`Amélie` becomes `amlie2001`). It is consistent on both sides, so
     it mostly still matches, but it loses information and can collide; accent-folding (NFKD) before the
     strip is the fix. It changes keys on both sides of every join, so it needs its own build and a
     before/after link count.
  Defect 1 is now guarded in the shipped matcher (`match_film` returns none for a title with no Latin
  letters, with a test that failed before the fix). Before it, the guard was only partial: candidates
  sharing a key were already dropped, but a lone non-Latin title could link to a lone stemless source record.
* **TMDB reviews pilot, run** (`experiments/tmdb_reviews_pilot.py`, 65 sampled films, one page each):
  films with **50 or more reviews: 0 in every bloc**. Films with any review: English 2021-26 8 of 15
  (4 with 5 or more), English to 2010 1 of 5, Hindi 1 of 15, Telugu 1 of 10, Japanese 2 of 10, French
  0 of 10. Median reviews is 0 or 1. **TMDB reviews cannot reach the 50-review floor for any language**, so
  they are not a source for full receipts; at most a small top-up. The recent-English gap (861 films at 50
  reviews from the dumps) and all four other blocs need a different source or the ratings-only tier.
* **Review-count weighting tested and rejected** (D-026): no gain on rated films.
